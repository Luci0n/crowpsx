Build a PS1-inspired look.
Keep your art direction.

Set up CrowPSX materials in Unity Built-in or URP, then tune vertex snapping, affine warping, lighting, posterization, foliage, and terrain.

By CrowBite, creator of CrowPSX Updated October 16, 20265 min read

THE WALKTHROUGH

01 / First material02 / Geometry03 / Lighting04 / World building05 / Check the result

Written by CrowBite for CrowPSX 1.1.0. Menu and control names follow the package documentation. This is an artistic workflow for a PS1-inspired appearance, not a claim of hardware-accurate emulation.

A convincing retro scene comes from the relationship between geometry, textures, lighting, and camera presentation. Start with one material and a simple scene. Add each imperfection deliberately so you can see what it contributes.

01 / Get one material working.

  1. Import CrowPSX and keep it at Assets/CrowPSX. Use Unity 2022.3 LTS or newer with Built-in or URP.
  2. Run Tools > CrowPSX > Validate Installation and resolve the reported setup issues.
  3. Create a material using CrowPSX/Surface/Lite for a reduced feature set or CrowPSX/Surface/Default for the fuller non-PBR workflow.
  4. Assign a Base Texture and Base Color. Apply the material to a mesh with clearly visible triangles and move the camera around it.

For a workflow with normal maps, metallic response, or reflections, choose CrowPSX/Surface/PBR. PBR is an artistic option; you do not need it simply to produce a retro appearance.

02 / Tune the geometry before the color.

Open Retro Geometry. Adjust Pixel Snap X/Y while moving the camera slowly. Look at the silhouette and nearby edges. Use the lowest strength that gives your scene the character you want.

Next, increase Affine Warp and watch a textured surface at an angle. This control reduces perspective-correct interpolation. Large triangles make the distortion more obvious, so mesh topology is part of the result.

If the near-camera motion becomes distracting, use Snap Fade Start/End. Judge the effect at the game's actual movement speed, rather than only in a still screenshot. In VR, use the separate snap and affine strength controls and test in the headset.

03 / Make light and color work together.

In Lighting, start with Vertex for a coarser appearance or Pixel for smoother per-pixel light. Adjust Lighting Steps to introduce bands. Use Final Posterize to reduce the completed lit result's color steps.

Change one control at a time. A dark wall can lose important shapes when lighting bands and posterization are both aggressive. Check an unlit corner, a midtone surface, and a bright light before applying the settings broadly.

Camera resolution, full-screen dithering, and CRT effects are separate presentation choices. The material suite should not be treated as a promise that all of those camera effects are included.

04 / Carry the look through the world.

Foliage

Choose a CrowPSX/Nature variant and assign the albedo texture. The documented starting defaults are Cutout with Cull Off. In Nature Wind, pick a preset and choose an anchoring source that matches the mesh. Local Height suits plants with a ground-level pivot; authored weights can use Vertex Alpha, UV2 Y, or a Mask Texture.

Terrain

On the Terrain object, select a Custom material using CrowPSX/Terrain/PBR. The shader reads Unity TerrainLayers. Keep the bundled terrain helper shaders installed so Built-in add passes and distant basemaps remain available.

Sky and scene accents

Assign CrowPSX/Skybox/Procedural under Lighting > Environment. Use its gradient, clouds, stars, and celestial controls to support the scene's palette. Add particles and depth-volume decals where they help the environment tell a story.

Under URP, depth-volume decals require the CrowPSX Depth Volume renderer feature. Optional mesh outlines need the CrowPSX Outline Feature or camera support component. Grab Distortion needs Opaque Texture enabled on the URP Asset.

05 / Check the result in motion.

  • Move through near and distant geometry; inspect snapping and affine distortion.
  • Check opaque, cutout, and transparent materials in their intended lighting.
  • Verify URP renderer features on the renderer your camera actually uses.
  • Profile the target build on its target hardware. A Lite material is a reduced feature option, not a guaranteed frame-rate improvement.
  • Save a material preset, then review the Material Manager's target list before applying it across your project.

The package includes a demo at Assets/CrowPSX/Demo.unity. Use it to inspect the author's setup, then validate a small scene from your own game.
